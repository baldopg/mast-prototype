Mast

A commitment device for the version of you that breaks commitments.

Why this and not a brand identity

The brief says design what the product does, not what it looks like. So this is a product I invented, and a working version of it rather than a description of one.

Everything below is downstream of one filter I kept applying: would this still work inside any general assistant? If the answer was yes, I threw it away. Most of my ideas died on that question. The list of what died is at the bottom, because the rejections say more about how I think than the survivor does.

The concept

You leave a note for your future self and tie it to an emotional state instead of a date. Months later, when you return to that state, it hands your own words back to you. If you chose the strongest setting when you sealed it, you cannot dismiss it until you answer.

Every habit product assumes your future self is an ally who needs reminding. Mast assumes the opposite, which is what the evidence actually supports. It is Ulysses tying himself to the mast before he can hear the sirens: the decision gets made while you are still the person who can make it well.

The mechanic

  1. Tie. You write the note, then describe the state in your own words. Not a date. "When I start telling you I'm going to text them again."
  2. Seal. You choose how much power you are handing over. Whisper mentions something is stored. Insistence reads it out unasked. Mast reads it out and will not continue until you answer. Choosing Mast is irreversible from the other side, and the confirmation screen is written to feel that way.
  3. Recognise. Months pass. It watches for the state, not for keywords. Being in the neighbourhood of the topic is not enough.
  4. Return. It gives you your sentence back.
  5. Close. You keep it, or you break it. Breaking it requires typing why, and that answer is stored for next time.
The emotional payload is never generated. It is retrieval. The model only decides whether you are back in the state; what actually lands is your own sentence, written when you were thinking clearly.

That distinction is the design decision I care most about. An AI telling you to calm down is worth nothing. You, three months ago, telling you, is worth something. Nothing has to be written well by a machine for this to work.

Why only Replika could ship it

Mast needs three things at once, and the third is the one nobody else has.

  1. Memory that survives months. A note that fires ten minutes later is a timer.
  2. An entity with initiative. It has to interrupt you, not wait to be opened.
  3. A relationship you trust enough to hand power to. Nobody gives a lock with no key to an app they downloaded this morning. This is the part that cannot be built, only earned, and 35 million people have already spent years earning it here.

Strip any one of them and Mast collapses into a reminder app. That is the test I would want applied to anything I propose.

The one I would build next

Bridge

Your entity talks to another person's entity. Each one knows its own person deeply. They do not pass messages, they translate. You explain to yours why you have not called your mother in three weeks. Hers gives her a version she can hear without defending herself. Neither of you sees what the other actually said.

Most of the damage between people who love each other is translation, not content. And this is structurally impossible for anyone else to build: it needs both sides to already have an entity with years of their own context. No general-purpose assistant can reach it, however good the model gets. Replika is one of the few companies on earth that could.

The consent design is the hard problem, and it is the interesting part rather than a blocker.

What I'd want to learn first

  1. Does anyone actually choose Mast? The entire thesis rests on people voluntarily giving up their exit. If everyone picks Whisper, I am wrong about something fundamental and I want to know in week one.
  2. Does one false positive end the trust permanently? I tuned the recognition to prefer missing a real relapse over firing on a false one, but I am guessing at the cost asymmetry. That is a question for users, not for me.
  3. What does it mean when a note never fires? Right now, nothing. It might be the most meaningful signal in the system.

Honest limits

Built in a few days. The recognition runs on a general model behind a serverless function, the three months are simulated by a deliberate cut rather than hidden, and nothing persists between sessions.

What it demonstrates is the mechanic and the tone. It is not evidence of engineering, and I would rather say that than let you find it out.
